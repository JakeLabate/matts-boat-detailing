Greenwich, Connecticut

Captain Harbor, Cos Cob Harbor, Byram Harbor

Greenwich is the western edge of our service area and about fifty minutes down I-95 from Milford. We do not come here for a single quick wash. We come for larger jobs, ceramic coating work, and owners who book several boats at one club or yard so the trip earns itself.

The town runs an unusual setup. Four resident marinas at Byram, Cos Cob, Grass Island, and the Greenwich Point boat yard operate from mid-April to mid-November on facility use permits, and alongside them sits a cluster of private clubs and commercial yards along the Mianus River in Cos Cob. Between the two you get a wider spread of boat sizes here than anywhere else we work.

Local conditions

Two very different problems in one town

The town marinas are full of small boats. Byram and Grass Island cap out around 21 to 22 feet, and Cos Cob at 28. These are open boats with no cabin top shading anything, and they take full sun on the gelcoat, seats, and non-skid all season. Oxidation shows up fast and a sealant is worth more than owners expect on a boat this size.

The clubs and Mianus River yards are the opposite. Larger vessels, deeper pockets, higher standards, and finishes that are expensive to correct once they go. On those boats we push hard for ceramic coating rather than repeat compounding, because the cost curve on a large hull only runs one direction. Captain Harbor is also genuinely exposed western Sound water, so salt spray is relentless out on the moorings.
