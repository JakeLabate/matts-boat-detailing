Stratford, Connecticut

Housatonic River, Long Island Sound

Stratford sits directly across the Housatonic River from us, which makes it the shortest run our truck makes. We are usually on site within about ten minutes of leaving Milford, and we detail more boats here than almost anywhere else on the coast.

The town has kept boats in the water since the shipyards of the 1700s, and the west bank of the river is still lined with working marinas. Whether your boat is slipped a mile and a half upriver or sitting on a trailer in your driveway near Short Beach, we bring the whole setup to you.

Local conditions

What the Housatonic does to a hull

The Housatonic is a tidal river, so boats kept here sit in brackish water that swings between fresh and salt with every cycle. That mix tends to leave a stubborn stain line right at the waterline, browner and harder to remove than what we see on boats kept out in open salt water.

Current in the river can run up to three knots, which keeps growth moving but also means silt and tannins are constantly working against your gelcoat. Boats slipped upriver usually need attention at the waterline more often than their owners expect, and a polymer sealant or ceramic coating buys real time between details here.
