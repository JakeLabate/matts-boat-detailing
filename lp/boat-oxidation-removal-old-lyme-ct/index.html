Why Old Lyme is like this

Local conditions, not a sales pitch.

the east bank of the Connecticut River, Great Island, and the Black Hall River

One of the prettier pieces of water in the state and one of the more productive, which is good for wildlife and hard on finishes.

The marshes around Great Island make this some of the most biologically productive water on the Connecticut coast, and productive water grows things on hulls. Combine that with the silt and tannin coming down the river and you get the heaviest, most set-in waterline staining we deal with anywhere. Boats in the creeks behind Great Island also sit over mud at low tide, which adds a stain of its own.
