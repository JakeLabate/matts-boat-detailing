Why this one is worth building. It qualifies as hard as it sells. Someone who sees $2,600 for a 40 footer and leaves was never going to book, and Matt gets that hour back. Wash plan season math

Eight dollars a foot per visit reads as small. The season total is the number owners actually decide on, so this shows it.

This one is an assumption, not a rate. Move it to match how long your boat is actually in the water.

This one changed my mind while I built it. I pitched this as showing the plan is cheaper than letting her go and paying for correction later. The real numbers do not support that. A 30 footer washed weekly runs about $5,760 a season against $1,950 for a one-off Buff & Shine. Only the monthly plan lands near the cost of a single correction. So the component shows the correction cost as a reference point and says plainly what you are buying: a boat that is clean every time you use her, not a saving. If you want the savings angle, it needs a discount on the plans, not better wording.

Component

Marina picker

Milford as the example. The outline is the same one already on the town pages, from Connecticut boundary data.

Where is she kept?

Every marina, club and yard we already service in Milford. Tap one and it goes into the form, so nobody has to type "Safe Harbor Port Milford" on a phone.

Picked: nothing yet

This is the one I could not build as pitched, and the reason matters. I proposed clickable pins on the town outline. When I went to place them, all 81 marina pages turned out to carry the identical coordinate 41.2306, -73.064 in their schema. Beacon Point in Greenwich and Dodson in Stonington are 80 miles apart and share a latitude and longitude. So there is no real position data to pin with, and I am not going to scatter markers at invented coordinates on a map that claims to be Milford. What is above is the same benefit without the fiction: the outline for local proof, and real marina names that prefill the field people abandon on. Worth fixing on the live site regardless of this component, since publishing false coordinates in Place schema across 81 pages is its own problem.
05
Component

Which service does she need

Three questions about the boat in front of you, then a straight answer, including when the answer is the cheap one.

Does water still bead on the hull sides?

Run a hand along the gelcoat. How does it feel?

When was she last waxed or coated?

What she probably needs

 

 

Answer the three and it will say which service fits. It can recommend something cheaper than the page you landed on, which is the point.

The decision this component forces, with the real numbers. I walked all 27 answer combinations. It recommends Wash & Wax on 8 of them, Buff & Shine on 9, oxidation removal on 9, and Ceramic Coating on exactly 1. That is what an honest triage looks like, because you only coat sound gelcoat that has nothing on it, and most boats fail one of those two tests. It also means that on a ceramic coating landing page this tool talks 26 visitors out of 27 into something cheaper, which is a real problem for the ad spend on that page. So it offers coating underneath the honest answer, once, framed as what it actually is: the way to stop repeating a seasonal job. If you would rather it never downsold, do not ship it. A triage that always recommends the expensive answer is a form with extra steps, and people can tell.
