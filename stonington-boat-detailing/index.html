Stonington, Connecticut

Stonington Harbor, Fishers Island Sound, Pawcatuck River

Stonington is the furthest point we travel, about eighty minutes east of Milford and hard against the Rhode Island line. It is Connecticut's easternmost cruising port and the only one with genuine Atlantic Ocean exposure, and locals will tell you it has the most beautiful harbor on the East Coast.

The Borough, incorporated in 1801 and the oldest in Connecticut, sits on a narrow peninsula and is home to the last remaining full-time commercial fishing fleet in the state. The Blessing of the Fleet, rooted in the Borough's Portuguese community, has run since 1954. This is a working harbor with a real fleet in it, not a resort.

Local conditions

Atlantic exposure is a different animal

Everything west of here is Long Island Sound, which is protected water whether owners think of it that way or not. Stonington is not. This harbor gets genuine ocean exposure, and boats kept here take a salt load that nothing on the rest of our coast matches. A finish that would hold two seasons in Milford will look tired in one here.

That makes Stonington the strongest case for ceramic coating in our entire range, and the place where we are least likely to recommend anything else. There is also a working fleet character to the harbor: commercial boats bring diesel, fish, and bait residue into the air and water, and pleasure boats moored nearby pick it up. Non-skid that has had bait residue worked into the texture needs proper cleaning rather than a rinse, and a sealed non-skid surface releases it far more easily next time.
